Gluten-Free in Korea: What to Avoid and What to Say

Open the free tool →

Rice is everywhere in Korea, but wheat hides in the sauces. Most soy sauce contains wheat, and gochujang and factory-made doenjang often contain wheat or barley. Celiac disease is not widely known, so explain clearly.

The phrase to show

저는 셀리악병이 있어서 밀, 보리, 호밀을 먹으면 크게 아파요.I have celiac disease. Wheat, barley and rye make me very sick.
간장, 고추장, 된장, 쌈장, 춘장에도 밀이 들어 있을 수 있어요.Soy sauce, gochujang, doenjang, ssamjang and chunjang can contain wheat.
밀가루 음식에 쓴 조리도구나 튀김 기름을 같이 쓰는 것도 위험해요.Cooking tools or frying oil also used for wheat foods are risky too.

Usually not safe

  • Anything fried or in pancake batter (jeon, twigim, fried chicken)
  • Noodles (most contain wheat; naengmyeon noodles are often buckwheat mixed with wheat)
  • Fish cake (eomuk) and wheat rice cakes (mil-tteok)
  • Barley tea (bori-cha), often served free, and sikhye, made with barley malt
  • Kimchi and other side dishes (some kimchi is made with wheat-flour paste; many are seasoned with soy sauce)

Often easier

Plain white rice (not japgok-bap, mixed-grain rice, which often contains barley), grilled meat without marinade, and plain rice cakes (check they are not mil-tteok). Ask every time: marinades and soups are often seasoned with soy sauce. Some travelers carry a small bottle of gluten-free soy sauce.

At convenience stores

Wheat must be listed in the allergen box (밀). Barley is not in the required list, so also check the ingredient list for 보리 (barley), 맥아 and 엿기름 (malt).
