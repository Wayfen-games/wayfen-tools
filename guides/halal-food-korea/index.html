Halal Food in Korea: How to Ask in Korean

Pork is common in Korean food, often hidden in stock, sausage or blood sausage, and many dishes are seasoned with cooking wine. A clear Korean sentence helps the staff check before they cook.

The phrase to show

저는 무슬림이에요.I am Muslim.
돼지고기와 햄, 소시지, 순대, 돼지기름, 돼지 육수처럼 돼지고기로 만든 것을 먹지 못해요.I can't eat pork or anything made from pork: ham, sausage, sundae (blood sausage), lard, pork stock.
술, 맛술, 미림이 들어간 음식도 안 돼요.Food made with alcohol, cooking wine or mirin is also not OK.
다른 고기는 할랄 인증 고기만 먹을 수 있어요.Other meat must be halal-certified.

Where pork hides

  • Stews and soups: kimchi-jjigae often has pork (ask even for tuna versions, as the stock may be pork); gamja-tang is pork-bone stew.
  • Army stew (budae-jjigae): ham and sausage.
  • Gimbap: often has ham.
  • Sundae: pork intestine and blood.
  • Black bean noodles (jjajangmyeon): pork in the sauce.

Finding restaurants

The Korea Tourism Organization has used four levels: Halal Certified, Self-Certified, Muslim Friendly and Pork Free. Only Halal Certified means an outside body (such as the Korea Muslim Federation) checked the kitchen; Muslim Friendly and Pork Free places may serve alcohol and other meat. The program's list may not be up to date, so confirm with the restaurant. Itaewon in Seoul, near the Seoul Central Mosque, has the most halal restaurants.
