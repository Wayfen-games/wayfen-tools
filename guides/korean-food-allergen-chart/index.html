Korean Food Allergen Chart: 40 Common Dishes

Korean recipes hide a lot: anchovy stock in stews, fish sauce in kimchi, wheat in soy sauce and gochujang, sesame oil in many dishes. This chart shows what to ask about in 40 dishes travelers often order.

● usually contains · ○ depends on the restaurant · blank = not usually. "Meat (not pork)" means beef, chicken or other meat, including meat broth. Shellfish includes shrimp, crab, clams, oysters and squid. Nuts includes peanuts and pine nuts. Wheat is marked (● or ○) when soy sauce, gochujang or doenjang is used, because most store-bought versions contain wheat; traditional doenjang and soup soy sauce may not. For celiac disease, barley (sometimes in samgyetang) is also marked in the wheat column.

How to use it

  • A blank cell is not a guarantee. Recipes change by restaurant and by region, so always confirm with the staff.
  • Cross-contact (shared oil, grills and pans) is common and not shown here.
  • For severe allergies, show a Korean sentence instead of pointing at a menu. The free tool below has cards for allergies, celiac disease, vegan, vegetarian, halal and more, with the same chart built in.
  • The chart is based on common recipes, not on checks at individual restaurants. It is not medical advice. In an emergency call 119, and carry your own prescribed medication.
  • For halal, the chart shows pork but not alcohol (cooking wine, mirin) or whether meat is halal-certified. Ask about those separately.
