Peanut and Tree Nut Allergy in Korea: Foods to Watch

Open the free tool →

Most Korean main dishes don't contain nuts, but peanuts and tree nuts show up in sauces, side dishes, garnishes and snacks, often in small amounts. Pine nuts (잣) are a common garnish that people don't think of as "nuts".

Sentences to show

저는 땅콩과 견과류에 심한 알레르기가 있어요.I have a severe allergy to peanuts and tree nuts.
땅콩, 호두, 잣, 아몬드, 캐슈너트, 피스타치오와 땅콩기름, 땅콩버터, 견과류 가루가 조금만 들어가도 위험해요.Even a little peanut, walnut, pine nut, almond, cashew, pistachio, peanut oil, peanut butter or nut powder is dangerous.
반찬이나 소스, 국물, 고명에 땅콩이나 견과류(잣 포함)가 들어가나요?Are there peanuts or tree nuts (including pine nuts) in the side dishes, sauce, broth or garnish?
땅콩·견과류 음식과 같은 기름이나 조리 도구를 쓰는 것도 위험해요.Sharing oil or cooking tools with peanut or nut dishes is also dangerous.

Before you trust any list: recipes change by restaurant, and side dishes, sauces and toppings are added without being listed on the menu. Frying oil and tools are often shared. This page is not medical advice.

Where nuts often hide

  • Fried chicken: some sauced chicken (yangnyeom or soy) is topped with crushed peanuts or sliced almonds. Sweet crispy chicken bites (닭강정) very often come with peanuts.
  • Side dishes: stir-fried anchovies (멸치볶음) and other sweet side dishes are often made with peanuts, almonds or walnuts.
  • Garnish: pine nuts on galbi, yukhoe, galbitang and some soups and porridges; pine nut porridge (잣죽) is made from them.
  • Street food and snacks: hotteok filling often has nuts and seeds; walnut cakes (호두과자), nut brittle (강정) and sweet rice cake (약밥) contain nuts.
  • Samgyetang: some versions add chestnuts, ginkgo nuts or pine nuts, and some well-known samgyetang restaurants blend walnuts, pine nuts, seeds or peanut powder into the broth. Ask before ordering.

Usually contains nuts

  • Ginseng chicken soup (삼계탕) — Whole chicken; some add chestnuts, jujubes, ginkgo nuts, pine nuts or barley; some well-known restaurants blend walnuts, pine nuts or peanut powder into the broth
  • Hotteok (sweet pancake) (호떡) — Wheat dough; seeds and nuts in filling

Depends on the restaurant (ask)

  • Korean fried chicken (치킨) — Wheat batter; some sauced (yangnyeom or soy) chicken is topped with crushed peanuts or sliced almonds; sauces may contain soy
  • Beef tartare (육회) — Raw beef, egg yolk, sesame oil; pine nuts sometimes; sometimes served with raw octopus (낙지)
  • Rice cakes (tteok) (떡) — Rice base; coatings may be soybean, sesame or nuts
  • Grilled ribs (galbi) (갈비) — Beef or pork ribs (ask which), soy sauce marinade, sesame oil; pine nuts on top sometimes
  • Short rib soup (galbitang) (갈비탕) — Beef rib broth, glass noodles; egg garnish, jujubes or pine nuts in some places

The other dishes in the Korean food allergen chart usually have no nuts in the dish itself, but still ask about side dishes and sauces.

If you react

Follow your own emergency plan: if you have been prescribed an epinephrine auto-injector, use it as instructed, then call 119 for an ambulance. The free tool below has an SOS card that asks staff to call 119. Always carry your prescribed medication with you.

Reading Korean labels

Korean packaged food must list 땅콩 (peanut), 호두 (walnut) and 잣 (pine nut) as allergens (Korean food labeling standard, current version; pine nuts were added in 2018). Other tree nuts are not required allergen labels, so check the ingredient list for 아몬드 (almond), 캐슈너트 (cashew), 피스타치오 (pistachio), 헤이즐넛 (hazelnut), 마카다미아 (macadamia) and 피칸 (pecan).
