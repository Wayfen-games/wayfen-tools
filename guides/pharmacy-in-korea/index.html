How to Buy Medicine at a Korean Pharmacy

Pharmacies in Korea are called 약국 (yak-guk). Look for a sign with the character 약. Pharmacists can recommend over-the-counter medicine for common problems.

Phrases for common symptoms

머리가 아파요.I have a headache.
열이 나요.I have a fever.
목이 아파요.I have a sore throat.
소화가 안 돼요.I have indigestion.
설사를 해요.I have diarrhea.
맞는 약을 추천해 주세요.Please recommend something suitable.

Questions to ask

하루에 몇 번, 몇 알씩 먹어요?How many times a day, and how many pills?
이 약을 먹으면 졸려요?Will this make me sleepy?

When pharmacies are closed

Many pharmacies close in the evening and on Sundays. Registered 24-hour convenience stores sell a few basic medicines: pain and fever relievers, cold medicine, digestive aids and pain patches. They do not sell medicine for diarrhea (as of November 2026).

Shopping and tax refunds

Look for a “Tax Free” or “Tax Refund” sign and show your passport. Many stores refund the tax on the spot for purchases under ₩1,000,000 each, up to ₩5,000,000 per trip; otherwise claim it at the airport kiosk. The minimum is usually ₩15,000 per receipt, but check with the store. (Rules in place since 2024; check current rules.) The tool's Shopping tab has Korean cards for tax refunds, sizes and colors.

If it is serious

Call 119 for an ambulance. The Korea Travel Hotline 1330 can help with interpretation (English, Chinese and Japanese 24 hours). This page helps you describe how you feel. It is not medical advice.
