Sesame Allergy in Korea: Sesame Oil, Seeds and What to Say

Open the free tool →

Sesame is one of the most common flavors in Korean cooking. Sesame oil (참기름) is mixed into side dishes, rice and marinades, and toasted sesame seeds (통깨, 깨소금) are sprinkled on top of many dishes as a finishing touch.

Sentences to show

저는 참깨에 알레르기가 있어요.I am allergic to sesame.
참기름, 깨소금, 통깨를 빼 주세요.Please leave out sesame oil, sesame salt and sesame seeds.
나물, 조미김, 비빔밥, 김밥에 들어가는 참기름도 위험해요.Sesame oil in namul side dishes, seasoned seaweed, bibimbap and gimbap is also dangerous.
고명으로 뿌리는 깨도 빼 주실 수 있나요?Could you also leave off the sesame seeds sprinkled on top?

Before you trust any list: sesame seeds are added as a garnish to many dishes that don't list them, including some stews, rice cakes and noodles, and dipping sauces often contain sesame oil. Shared tools and pans are common. This page is not medical advice.

Where sesame often hides

  • Side dishes (banchan): namul (seasoned vegetables) are usually dressed with sesame oil and seeds.
  • Seasoned seaweed (조미김): the roasted seaweed snacks and the seaweed served with rice are usually brushed with sesame oil, perilla oil or a blend that includes them.
  • Rice dishes: bibimbap and gimbap are finished with sesame oil; fried rice and porridge often are too.
  • Marinades and dips: bulgogi, galbi and yukhoe marinades, and the salt-and-sesame-oil dip served with grilled pork.
  • Garnish: a pinch of sesame seeds on top of stir-fries, soups and even tteokbokki is very common.

Perilla (들깨, deulkkae) seeds and oil are a different plant from sesame (참깨). Whether perilla is safe for you is a question for your doctor; Korean menus sometimes just say 깨, which can mean either.

44 common dishes, by sesame

Based on common recipes.

Usually contains sesame

  • Bibimbap (비빔밥) — Beef, fried egg, gochujang (spicy - ask for it on the side), sesame oil; the meat topping is sometimes pork
  • Gimbap (김밥) — Ham, fish cake, imitation crab (may contain crab), egg, sesame oil
  • Japchae (잡채) — Beef or pork, soy sauce, sesame oil, egg garnish; may use oyster sauce
  • Bulgogi (불고기) — Beef, soy sauce, sesame oil; pork versions (돼지불고기, 고추장불고기) are common, so ask
  • Pork belly BBQ (삼겹살 + 쌈장) — Ssamjang (soy, wheat), sesame oil dip
  • Seaweed soup (미역국) — Beef or clams, soup soy sauce, sesame oil
  • Namul side dishes (나물 반찬) — Sesame oil, soy sauce; some use fish sauce; some use salted shrimp
  • Beef tartare (육회) — Raw beef, egg yolk, sesame oil; pine nuts sometimes; sometimes served with raw octopus (낙지)
  • Abalone porridge (전복죽) — Abalone, sesame oil
  • Grilled ribs (galbi) (갈비) — Beef or pork ribs (ask which), soy sauce marinade, sesame oil; pine nuts on top sometimes

Depends on the restaurant (ask)

These may get a sesame seed garnish, sesame oil, or a dipping sauce or side with sesame, depending on the restaurant.

  • Kimchi (김치) — Fish sauce, salted shrimp; some add oysters or wheat/rice paste
  • Soft tofu stew (순두부찌개) — Egg cracked in; seafood or meat
  • Tteokbokki (떡볶이) — Fish cake, anchovy stock, gochujang; wheat cakes common; sesame seeds often sprinkled on top
  • Seafood pancake (해물파전) — Wheat batter with egg, squid, shrimp; dipping sauce may have sesame
  • Kimchi pancake (김치전) — Wheat batter, kimchi (fish sauce); dipping sauce may have sesame
  • Cold noodles (냉면) — Beef broth (some places mix in pork), boiled egg half, sliced meat on top may be pork; noodles: buckwheat (메밀) and wheat
  • Knife-cut noodles (칼국수) — Wheat noodles, anchovy or clam broth; some places use beef bone broth
  • Ginseng chicken soup (삼계탕) — Whole chicken; some add chestnuts, jujubes, ginkgo nuts, pine nuts or barley; some well-known restaurants blend walnuts, pine nuts or peanut powder into the broth
  • Spicy pork stir-fry (제육볶음) — Pork, gochujang, soy sauce; may use oyster sauce
  • Boiled pork wraps (보쌈) — Served with salted shrimp and kimchi (often oysters)
  • Sundae (blood sausage) (순대) — Pork intestine, pig blood, glass noodles; often served with a salted shrimp dip, so ask for salt instead
  • Ramyeon (라면) — Wheat noodles; soup powder often has beef or seafood
  • Korean fried chicken (치킨) — Wheat batter; some sauced (yangnyeom or soy) chicken is topped with crushed peanuts or sliced almonds; sauces may contain soy
  • Pork bone stew (감자탕) — Pork spine, perilla seed powder (perilla is a different plant from sesame; ask if unsure)
  • Hotteok (sweet pancake) (호떡) — Wheat dough; seeds and nuts in filling
  • Rice cakes (tteok) (떡) — Rice base; coatings may be soybean, sesame or nuts
  • Spicy stir-fried chicken (dakgalbi) (닭갈비) — Chicken, gochujang, cabbage, rice cakes; cheese is often added; may use oyster sauce
  • Spicy beef soup (yukgaejang) (육개장) — Shredded beef, fernbrake, green onion, chili; egg sometimes stirred in
  • Rice cake soup (tteokguk) (떡국) — Beef or anchovy stock, egg garnish, seaweed, sesame; dumplings are often added
  • Dumplings (mandu) (만두) — Wheat wrappers; usually pork, tofu and vegetables; some have kimchi or shrimp
  • Braised chicken (jjimdak) (찜닭) — Chicken braised in soy sauce with glass noodles and vegetables; often a little spicy; may use oyster sauce
  • Pork soup with rice (dwaeji-gukbap) (돼지국밥) — Pork bone broth and sliced pork; often served with salted shrimp and a spicy paste to add; kimchi and side dishes with fish sauce come with it; some mix in beef bone broth
  • Sundae soup (sundae-guk) (순대국) — Pork broth with sundae (pig intestine, blood, glass noodles) and offal; perilla seed powder and salted shrimp often added; kimchi and side dishes with fish sauce come with it; some mix in beef bone broth
  • Bean sprout soup with rice (kongnamul-gukbap) (콩나물국밥) — Anchovy broth with soybean sprouts; often a raw egg; seasoned with salted shrimp; some add beef or squid
  • Hangover soup (haejang-guk) (해장국) — Many kinds: beef bone broth with congealed ox blood (seonji), pork bone, dried pollack, or bean sprout with anchovy broth; usually soybean paste and chili; some add egg, clams or oysters

Usually no sesame in the recipe

A garnish of sesame seeds or a sesame-oil dip can still be added. Ask every time.

  • Soybean paste stew (된장찌개) — Anchovy stock; beef, pork or clams often added; often has chili, so it can taste spicy
  • Kimchi stew (김치찌개) — Usually pork, sometimes tuna; kimchi has fish sauce and salted shrimp
  • Army stew (부대찌개) — Ham, sausage, kimchi (fish sauce, salted shrimp), stock, ramyeon, cheese
  • Beef bone soup (설렁탕 / 곰탕) — Beef bone broth; wheat noodles sometimes added
  • Fish cake (어묵 / 오뎅) — Fish paste with wheat flour
  • Black bean noodles (짜장면) — Pork, wheat noodles, chunjang (soy, wheat); some add seafood or a fried egg
  • Spicy seafood noodles (짬뽕) — Seafood, wheat noodles; usually stir-fried with pork; broth is meat stock or seafood/anchovy stock
  • Fish-shaped bread (붕어빵) — Wheat batter with egg; red bean or custard filling
  • Short rib soup (galbitang) (갈비탕) — Beef rib broth, glass noodles; egg garnish, jujubes or pine nuts in some places

If you react

Follow your own emergency plan: if you have been prescribed an epinephrine auto-injector, use it as instructed, then call 119 for an ambulance. The free tool below has a sesame card and an SOS card that asks staff to call 119. On Korean packaged food, sesame is not one of the required allergen labels (Korean food labeling standard, current version), so check the ingredient list for 참깨, 참기름 and 깨. Restaurant food usually has no allergen label at all, so ask.
